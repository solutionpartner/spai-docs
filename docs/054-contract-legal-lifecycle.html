Phase 20 — Contract & Legal Lifecycle

Purpose

Phase 20 makes legal readiness a first-class Control Plane state. It does not turn SolutionPartner into a qualified trust service provider. SolutionPartner owns the client experience, document lifecycle, versioning, workflow, evidence registry and onboarding gate; a selected external trust/signature provider performs the regulated signature/trust operations appropriate to the requested signature level.

Canonical flow

CRM / proposal
   -> customer_ref
   -> legal requirements (MSA, DPA, AI Schedule, SLA, connector authorisation...)
   -> immutable template version
   -> rendered PDF + SHA-256
   -> contract envelope
   -> Trust Provider Gateway
   -> signer authentication/signature
   -> authenticated provider webhook
   -> signed artifact + provider evidence reference
   -> tamper-evident event chain
   -> immutable evidence manifest
   -> Legal Gate = READY
   -> billing / onboarding / tenant deployment

Architectural boundary

SolutionPartner does: portal/UI, template/version lifecycle, hashes, routing, business approvals, HMAC-authenticated provider callbacks, storage references, audit/evidence manifest, legal requirements, onboarding gating.

Trust provider does: identity/signature operations, qualified or advanced trust functions as contracted, timestamps/certificates/evidence where applicable. Provider-specific secrets live in Vault/Secret Manager.

Legal baseline for design (not legal advice)

  • LSSI (Spain), Article 23: electronic contracts can have legal effect when consent and the other validity requirements are met; electronic form can satisfy a writing requirement, subject to specific exceptions/formalities.
  • eIDAS Regulation, Article 25: an electronic signature cannot be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic or not qualified; a qualified electronic signature has the equivalent legal effect of a handwritten signature.
  • eIDAS Article 26 defines requirements for advanced electronic signatures.
  • Spain Law 6/2020 regulates certain aspects of trust services and distinguishes qualified/non-qualified trust-service regimes.
  • Qualified status must be checked against the EU/national Trusted Lists for the specific service, not assumed from a vendor's marketing name.
  • GDPR applies to signer/contact/evidence personal data; minimise the data captured and protect it according to risk.

Production counsel must decide the appropriate signature level and formalities per document/use case.

Domain states

Template: draft -> active -> deprecated -> retired (version immutable once registered).

Envelope: draft -> pending_signature -> signed | declined | expired | void.

Legal requirement: pending -> satisfied based on a signed envelope of the required document type.

Immutability

  • A template version is never overwritten; publish version N+1.
  • A contract stores the SHA-256 of the exact PDF sent for signature.
  • Signature/provider events are appended into a chained hash log.
  • When signed, the system creates one evidence manifest with its own SHA-256.
  • The original provider evidence remains external/immutable; the Control Plane stores a reference and verifies its own evidence chain.

Onboarding gate

SP_REQUIRE_LEGAL_GATE=1 is required in production. ready=false if no requirements are configured or any required document remains unsigned. This is intentionally fail-closed.

RBAC

  • legal_admin: contracts.* plus read-only access to tenant/onboarding/security context.
  • Platform/release administrators: full contract lifecycle as configured.
  • Operators/auditors/viewers: contracts.read only.
  • Trust-provider webhook: no human RBAC; HMAC-authenticated transport and idempotent provider event IDs.

Production P0s before first real signature

  1. Select provider and confirm its status/services in the EU Trusted List where qualified services are required.
  2. Complete security/vendor/DPA/subprocessor review of the provider.
  3. Implement and test provider adapter (create/cancel/fetch evidence).
  4. Store provider credentials only in Vault/secret refs.
  5. Finalise counsel-approved contract templates and signature-level matrix.
  6. Use immutable/object storage with retention/hold policy for original and signed PDFs.
  7. Verify webhook authentication method required by the chosen provider; HMAC is the reference implementation, not a substitute for provider-specific controls.
  8. Define retention, deletion, legal hold and data-subject handling for evidence metadata.
  9. Test signature cancellation, expiry, duplicate webhooks, provider outage and evidence retrieval.
  10. Run the Phase 20 PAT before enabling SP_REQUIRE_LEGAL_GATE=1 for production onboarding.
